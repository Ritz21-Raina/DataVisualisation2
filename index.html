Birth Rate & Trends in Malaysia
Exploring birth patterns, ethnic trends, and demographic shifts across Malaysian states
Birth Rate Overview
Year 2000

Compared to all the given maps, we can see the birthrate is higher over here. The highest birth rate is found in Selangor with 3.99 births per 1000 people.

Year 2010

There is slight decrease in the birthrate, however, Selangor still stays at the top. Johor has a drastic change compared to the year 2000. It has dropped from 2.93 per 1000 to 1.96 per 100.

Year 2020

The birth rate has dropped drastically over the past 20 years. And the highest birth rate is 2.76 in Selangor.

Trends in Birth Counts by Ethnicity

This line chart shows the trend of live births in Malaysia by ethnicity from 2000 to 2023.

Overall, Bumiputera Malays consistently recorded the highest number of births, peaking around 2015–2017 before showing a gradual decline in recent years. The Chinese population exhibits a steady and notable downward trend, with a sharper decline observed after 2016, as highlighted in the chart.

Indian and Other Bumiputera groups have relatively stable but lower birth counts, with slight fluctuations over the years.

Birth Trends Across States

Selangor, Johor and Sabah remain at top 3 throughout the years.

On the other hand Labuan and Perlis come at the bottom. Putrajaya comes out in 2010, and takes the second last spot of Perlis, pushing Perlis a step higher.

Birth Counts by Gender and State (Over the Years)

Birth counts across states show nearly equal values for males and females. This indicates a naturally balanced gender ratio in live births.

The trend suggests consistency in fertility patterns across all regions. No state shows significant deviation between male and female births.

Overall, Malaysia maintains demographic stability despite declining birth rates. Both genders contribute evenly to the nation’s population growth.

Birth Count Distribution by Ethnicity and State

Selangor records the highest births across both genders, reflecting strong urban growth and concentration.

Sarawak follows closely, driven by its large Bumiputera Other population.

Smaller states like Perlis, Labuan, and Putrajaya show lower birth counts due to limited populations.

Across Malaysia, Bumiputera Malays and Bumiputera Other dominate, while Chinese, Indian, and other groups show lower figures.
